Cheap dayThere is no combined pass in Brindisi, and you don't need one — the museum and the cathedral are free. Your whole day of tickets is about €6, plus small change for the boat and the bus. Bring coins.

A compact Roman-and-Crusader old town on foot in the morning, the public motobarca across the harbour to climb the great stone rudder, then the line 4 bus north to the litoranea for a swim. Tap a stop to find it on the map; tap its number to check it off as you go.

Tickets & what things cost

Almost everything here is free. The Museo Ribezzo (free), the Duomo (free) and Palazzo Granafei-Nervegna (free) cost nothing at all. You pay only at San Giovanni al Sepolcro (~€3) and the Monumento al Marinaio (€3, children 11–18 €1) — about €6 all day. Transport is the same STP urban ticket for boat and bus: ~€1.10–1.20 a ride, ~€1.50 bought on board. The motobarca machine takes coins only, no change given — buy a few tickets at a tabaccheria, or use the MyCicero / Mooney app (choose STP Brindisi urbano).

Nothing here needs pre-booking — there are no timed entries and no queues. The only thing worth doing in advance is putting €6–8 in coins in your pocket.

The bus is the one thing that can bite. The motobarca is a proper urban line running every ~20 min all day, so it's safe. Line 4 is the summer beach line and runs roughly hourly — confirm the return time with the driver the moment you board, and if it's wrong for you, a taxi back into town is ten minutes. If you'd rather not gamble: drop the beach entirely (see Plan B in the footer) — Brindisi's town is worth a slow lunch on the lungomare instead.

Getting inCruise ships normally berth at Costa Morena, ~4 km out along a lorry road with no pavement — do not walk it. Take the ship shuttle (reported free on MSC calls, ~10 min, drops near Corso Garibaldi / the lungomare). MSC's own port page also refers to a cruise terminal with its entrance on via Spalato inside the inner harbour, which would be a 15-minute walk from the Roman Columns — check which berth you're on when you wake up.

RouteShuttle → Columns → Duomo → Ribezzo → Granafei-Nervegna → San Giovanni al Sepolcro · motobarca across to Casale → Marinaio · line 4 north → swim → line 4 back → shuttle.

Day mattersThis plan assumes a Friday call. The Marinaio is shut on Wednesdays (except cruise days) and only opens 09:00–13:00 on Tue/Thu; San Giovanni al Sepolcro shuts on Wednesdays; the Duomo closes 12:30–16:30; the Ribezzo shuts on Mondays. If the day shifts, re-check those four.

Plan B — no beachSkip stops 9–11 and you have an unhurried town day: add the Fontana Tancredi, the Castello Svevo from the outside (it's a live naval base — façade only), a long lunch of orecchiette and burrata on the lungomare, and the Marinaio at leisure. Brindisi's old centre is genuinely a two-to-three hour place; the beach is what fills the rest.

BringCoins for the boat and bus. Water shoes — the litoranea is sand mixed with rock. A hat: mid-July here runs ~31 °C and the old town has little shade before the Duomo.
